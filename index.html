The "Pot Lid" Effect: Why Delhi Can't Breathe at Night

Normally, warm air from the city rises up into the cold sky like hot air in a balloon, carrying smoke away. In winter nights, the opposite happens: The ground cools down fast, while a layer of warm air sits above it like a heavy lid on a cooking pot. Because warm air is lighter than cold ground air, the smoke cannot rise. All car fumes, dust, and farm smoke are sealed into a thin 150-meter space near the ground.

THERMODYNAMIC INVERSION EXPLAINER

1. Radiation Inversion in the Delhi Bowl

During Delhi's calm winter nights, longwave radiative cooling drops surface temperatures rapidly to 11-14°C. Warm air aloft creates a lid: $\frac{dT}{dz} > 0$. Colder surface air cannot rise because it is denser than the air above.

2. Why Stubble Smoke Gets Trapped

Smoke advecting from Punjab and Haryana enters Delhi NCR at 100-250m altitude. When it encounters the capping inversion, it cannot disperse vertically and is reflected directly into the ground breathing zone (fumigation).

3. The Aerosol Feedback Multiplier

Heavy soot and particulate matter in the smog layer absorb sunlight aloft while shielding the ground from solar warming. This reinforces the temperature inversion, delaying the morning breakdown by up to 3.5 hours.

🔬 Scientific Capabilities & Information Guide

Comprehensive breakdown of what the AERIS-DELHI engine tracks and models.

01. Pollutant Science

PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃: Tracks microscopic particulate matter and toxic gases. PM2.5 penetrates deep into lungs (from combustion), NO₂ inflames airways (from vehicles), and surface O₃ forms via sunlight-driven chemical reactions causing severe smog.

02. Why AQI Changes

Meteorological Drivers: High wind disperses pollutants. Low temperature & high humidity create dense, stagnant air masses. Rainfall causes "wet deposition," washing particulates out of the sky. Atmospheric mixing distributes smoke vertically.

03. Pollution Sources

Primary Emitters: Vehicular exhaust (NO₂, PM2.5), construction dust (PM10), industrial stacks (SO₂), biomass/stubble burning (PM2.5 spikes), and localized domestic combustion.

04. Source Apportionment

Estimated Contributions: Algorithmically isolates local urban emissions (base load) from advected agricultural smoke (stubble share) to apportion responsibility dynamically in real-time.

05. PM2.5 vs PM10

Particle Size Comparison: PM10 (dust/pollen) gets trapped in the upper respiratory tract. PM2.5 is 1/30th the width of a human hair and can cross the alveolar barrier directly into the bloodstream.

06. Weather–Pollution Relationship

Coupled Dynamics: Temperature drops compress air. Low wind speed (< 2 m/s) stops horizontal flushing. High humidity swells hygroscopic aerosols, drastically reducing visibility.

07. Planetary Boundary Layer

The Pot Lid Effect: During winter nights, the PBL collapses from 2000m down to 100m. Thermal inversions form a physical "lid" over the city, trapping all localized emissions near the surface.

08. Pollution Transport Map

Wind Vectors: Tracks North-Westerly winds transporting agricultural smoke from Punjab/Haryana down into the Delhi NCR basin via the primary regional air corridors.

09. Seasonal Pollution Pattern

Annual Cycle: Summer (Dust storms) → Monsoon (Washout/Clean) → Post-Monsoon (Stubble burning crisis) → Winter (Intense inversion trapping & localized smog).

10. 24-Hour Pollution Cycle

Diurnal Flux: AQI drops in the afternoon as solar radiation breaks the inversion. AQI spikes severely between 10 PM and 6 AM as the ground cools rapidly, trapping emissions.

11. 3-Day Scientific Forecast

Predictive Modeling: Uses WRF-Chem atmospheric physics coupled with real-time Open-Meteo telemetry to project hour-by-hour PM2.5 accumulation up to T+72h.

12. Pollution Events

Anomaly Detection: Algorithmically flags unusual AQI spikes (e.g., Diwali fireworks, sudden localized industrial fires, or massive stubble influxes) using dynamic rate-of-change metrics.

13. Scientific Glossary

Definitions: Aerosols (suspended particles), Inversion (warm air trapping cold air), NAAQS (National Ambient Air Quality Standards), SAFAR (System of Air Quality and Weather Forecasting And Research).

14. Research & Evidence

Literature Backing: Aerosol dynamics and dispersion modeling parameters are based on peer-reviewed meteorological guidelines from the CPCB and global environmental agencies.

15. Methodology

System Architecture: AERIS pulls from Open-Meteo CAMS (Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service), calculating localized physics-driven severity across 15 CAAQMS coordinates simultaneously.
